# Run The Study Loop

Use the system to reduce friction. Do not use it to replace problem solving.

## Before The Lecture: 10 Minutes

1. Read the title and section names.
2. Write the prerequisite terms that you cannot define.
3. Ask for one small example of each prerequisite.
4. Read the lecture's final theorem or algorithm statement.
5. Write one question that the lecture should answer.

## During The Lecture

Keep one short note for each semantic section:

```text
Builds on:
Main problem:
Key invariant:
Why it is correct:
Why it is fast:
Trap or edge case:
```

Pause when the proof changes direction. Do not copy every line. Record the invariant and the move that preserves it.

## After The Lecture: 20 Minutes

1. Explain the algorithm without looking at notes.
2. Rebuild one proof from its invariant.
3. Solve one small example by hand.
4. Mark gaps as `now`, `deeper-later`, or `reference`.
5. Add corrected terms and chapter notes to the local index.

## Use AI As A Tutor

Give the model the local transcript, the relevant slides, and your current explanation. Ask the model to test your understanding.

```text
Use only the attached course material.
Ask one diagnostic question at a time.
Do not reveal the answer until I commit to an approach.
When I am wrong, name the first incorrect assumption.
Finish with one transfer problem that changes the surface details.
```

For graded work, use AI to check a completed attempt or explain prerequisite ideas. Do not use it to impersonate your work. Keep answer keys out of the active problem-solving context.
